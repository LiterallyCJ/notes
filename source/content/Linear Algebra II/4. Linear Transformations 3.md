### Getting ker(L)

To find $ker(L)$, we just have to get the null space. This is done by multiplying the matrix of the transformation by a vector and setting it equal to 0. For example,

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & 4 & -2
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z \\
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{gather*}
x + 2y - z = 0 \\
2x + 4y - 2z = 0
\end{gather*}
$$
$$
x = -2y + z
$$
$$
let\ y = s, let\ z = t
$$
$$
x = -2s + t
$$
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2s + t \\
s \\
t
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
-2s + t \\
s \\
t
\end{pmatrix}
=
s
\begin{pmatrix}
-2 \\
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
+ t
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
0 \\
1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
So,
$$
ker(A) = Span \left(
\begin{pmatrix}
-2 \\
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix} ,
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
0 \\
1
\end{pmatrix}
\right)
$$
To get  $dim(ker(A))$, just count the number of vectors in the span.

### Getting im(L)

To find $im(L)$, we need to find all possible outputs (the column space). To do this, we put all of the columns as vectors in a span and turn it into a minimal spanning set.

$$
A = 
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & 4 & -2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
So we try,
$$
im(A) = Span \left(
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\ 2
\end{pmatrix}, 
\begin{pmatrix}
2 \\ 4
\end{pmatrix}, 
\begin{pmatrix}
-1 \\ -2
\end{pmatrix}
\right)
$$
But we can see that the second and third columns are multiples of the first:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\begin{pmatrix}
2 \\ 4
\end{pmatrix}
=
2
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\ 2
\end{pmatrix} \\
\text{and} \\
\begin{pmatrix}
-1 \\ -2
\end{pmatrix}
= -
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\ 2
\end{pmatrix}
\end{gather*}
$$
So we have 
$$
im(A) = Span \left(
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\ 2
\end{pmatrix}
\right)
$$
