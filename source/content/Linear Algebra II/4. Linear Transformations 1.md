An $m \times n$ matrix $A$ over a field $F$ is an array
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
\vdots & & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
We use notation $M_{m \times n}(F)$ for the set of all $(m \times n)$ matrices over $F$.

### Linear Transformations

A linear A map $L : V \to W$ is called a linear transformation if the following two conditions hold:

* $\forall\ x, y \in V, L(x + y) = L(x) + L(y)$
* $\forall\ a \in F, x \in V, L(ax) = a(L(x))$

### Matrix Representation

Let $F$ be a field. Let $L: F^n \to F^m$ be a linear transformation.
There exists a unique matrix $A \in M_{m \times n}(F)$ such that $L = L_A$.

For example,
$$
\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2, \pmatrix{c_1 \\ c_2 \\ c_3} \to \pmatrix{2c_1 + c_3 - 4c_2 \\ c_2}
$$
can be represented as the matrix
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -4 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\in M_{2 \times 3}(\mathbb{R})
$$
### Kernel and image

Let $L: V \to W$ be a linear transformation between vector spaces $V$, $W$, over a field $F$.
Then,
$$
ker(L) := \{x \in V: L(x) = 0_W\}
$$
Basically the kernel is the set of all input vectors that become 0

$$
im(L) := \{y \in W: \exists x \in V : y = L(x)\}
$$
Basically the image is the set of all outputs of $L$

To simplify things,
$$
ker(L_A) = N(A)
$$
where $N(A)$ denotes the null space of $A$
