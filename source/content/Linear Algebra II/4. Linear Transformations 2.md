### Kernel and Image as Subspaces

If $L: V \to W$,
$$
\begin{gather*}
ker(L) \text{ is a subspace of } V \\
im(L) \text{ is a subspace of } W
\end{gather*}
$$

Let $V$ and $W$ be vector space over a field $F$ and let $L: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. Let $x_1, \dots, x_n \in V$. Then:
* If $x_1, \dots, x_n \in V$ span $V$, then $L(x_1), \dots, L(x_n)$ span $im(L)$
* If $L(x_1), \dots, L(x_n)$ are linearly independent, then $x_1, \dots, x_n$ are linearly independent
### The Kernel Criterion

A linear transformation $L : V \to W$ is injective iff:
$$
ker(L) = \{0_V\}
$$
> Basically meaning the only vector that gets sent to 0 is the zero vector.

### Isomorphism

Let $V, W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$. A bijective linear transformation $L: V \to W$ is called an isomorphism. 
The vector spaces are called isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism $L: V \to W$; we then write $V \cong W$

Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $F$ of dimension $n$ and $m$. $V$ and $W$ are isomorphic iff $n = m$

### Dimension Theorem

Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $F$ of finite dimension and let $L: V \to W$ be a linear transformation from $V$ to another vector space $W$ over $F$, then:

$$
dim_F(ker(L)) + dim_F(im(L)) = dim_F(V)
$$
This is also known as the rank-nullity theorem
