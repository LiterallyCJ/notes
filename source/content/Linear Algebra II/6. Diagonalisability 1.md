### Eigenspaces

Let $L: V \to V$ be a linear transformation. For a scalar $\lambda \in F$, the eigenspace is:
$$
E_\lambda(L) = \{x \in V : L(X) = \lambda x\}
$$

This means that $E_\lambda(X)$ contains all vectors that are scaled by $L$ instead of being moved to a different direction

A scalar $\lambda$ is called an eigenvalue if there is a non-zero vector $x$ such that $L(X) = \lambda x$. So for the matrix a, $Ax = \lambda x$

### Eigenspaces as subspaces

$E_\lambda(L)$ is always a subspace of $V$.
$$
\begin{gather*}
Ax = \lambda x \\
(\lambda I_n - A)x = 0 \\
E_\lambda(A) = N(\lambda I_n - A)
\end{gather*}
$$

### Characteristic polynomial

If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $det(\lambda I_n - A) = 0$.
The characteristic polynomial,
$$
p_A(\lambda) = det(\lambda I_n - A)
$$

To get eigenvalues:
1. Form $\lambda I_n - A$
2. Compute $det(\lambda I_n - A)$
3. Solve $p_A(\lambda) = 0$
