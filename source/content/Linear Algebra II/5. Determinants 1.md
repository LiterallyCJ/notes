### Leibniz' definition of determinant

Let $F$ be a field. Let $n \geq 1$ and $A = (a_{ij})_{i , j = 1, \dots, n} \ in M_{n \times n}(F)$. Then:
$$
det(A):= \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} sgn(\sigma) \prod^n_{i = 1}a_{i, \sigma(i)} \in F
$$
is called the determinant of $A$.

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix the determinant is:
$$
\begin{gather*}
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{pmatrix} \\ \\
det(A) = ad - bc
\end{gather*}
$$

For a $3 \times 3$ matrix the determinant is:
$$
\begin{gather*}
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
​a_{11}​ & a_{21} & ​a_{31}​​ \\

\end{gather*}
$$
For a triangular matrix the determinant is:
$$
\begin{gather*}
A = 
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b & c \\
0 & d & e \\
0 & 0 & f 
\end{pmatrix} \\ \\
det(A) = a \times d \times f
\end{gather*}
$$

### Weierstrass' axiomatic description of the determinant map

Let $F$ be a field. Let $n \geq 1$. The map
$$
det: M_{n \times n}(F) \to F, A \to det(A)
$$
has the following properties and is uniquely determined by these properties:

1. det is linear in each column
$$
det
\begin{pmatrix}
 & a_{1s} + b_{1s} & \\
 C & \vdots & D \\
  & a_{ns} + b_{ns}
\end{pmatrix}
=
det
\begin{pmatrix}
 & a_{1s} & \\
 C & \vdots & D \\
  & a_{ns}
\end{pmatrix}
+
det
\begin{pmatrix}
 & b_{1s} & \\
 C & \vdots & D \\
  & b_{ns}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
2. Multiplying any column of a matrix $A \in M_{n \times n}(F)$ with a scalar $\lambda \in F$ changes $det(A)$ by the factor $\lambda$
$$
det
\begin{pmatrix}
 & \lambda a_{1s} & \\
 C & \vdots & D \\
  & \lambda a_{ns}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\lambda \cdot det
\begin{pmatrix}
 & a_{1s} & \\
 C & \vdots & D \\
& a_{ns}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
3. If two columns of $A \in M_{n \times n}(F)$ are equal, then $det(A) = 0$
4. $det(I_n) = 1$

> Rows and columns are interchangeable

1. For all $\lambda \in F$ we have $det(\lambda A) = \lambda \cdot det(A)$
2. If a column $A$ is the 0 column, then $det(A) = 0$
3. Let $B$ be obtained from $A$ by swapping the columns of $A$. Then $det(B) = - det(A)$
4. Let $\lambda \in F$ and $B$ be obtained from $A$ by adding the $\lambda$-multiple of the $j^{th}$ column of $A$ to the $i^{th}$ column of $A$ ($i \neq j$). Then $det(B) = det(A)$
